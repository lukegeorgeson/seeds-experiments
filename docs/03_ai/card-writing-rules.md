# Card Writing Rules

## Purpose

Define the output standard for card text across all generation modes.

## Core rule

Each card should express one idea clearly enough to be useful and briefly enough to stay combinable.

## Writing rules

- one idea per card
- short phrase or short sentence, not a paragraph
- concrete enough to be meaningful
- direct enough to feel like a working note
- distinct from neighboring cards

## Style guidance

Prefer:

- crisp strategic fragments
- blunt notebook phrasing
- practical observations
- specific levers
- concrete objects, gestures, rituals, behaviors, or formats
- plain English over polished copy

Avoid:

- abstract filler
- management-speak
- repeated sentence structures
- obvious paraphrases of the brief
- polished deck headlines
- poetic or atmospheric lines that sound smarter than they are
- stacked clauses when one shorter note would do

## Length guidance

Recommended v1 decision:

- target roughly 2 to 12 words per card
- allow occasional longer cards only when clarity clearly requires it

## Practicality bias

Recommended v1 decision:

- cards should feel easy to sort, edit, combine, or challenge
- early rounds should sound more like a strategist's notebook than a keynote deck
- if a card sounds quotable but not actionable, it is too polished

## Column sensitivity

- `Signals` should sound like observations, tensions, or behaviors
- `Nuggets` should sound like useful ingredients or linkages
- `Directions` should name clear territories or frames
- `Ideas` should sound activatable or makeable
- `Modifiers` should sound like portable twists, constraints, or finishing moves

## Validation rules

- reject cards that duplicate another card too closely
- reject cards that contain more than one major clause if they split into separate ideas
- reject cards that are too vague to act as signal
- reject cards that mostly mirror prompt language without adding a new angle

## Editing implications

If a user rewrites a card, preserve their language in later prompts as a higher-confidence signal rather than paraphrasing it away.

## Future-facing notes

- later versions can support house styles or brand tones, but v1 should prioritize clarity over stylistic mimicry
