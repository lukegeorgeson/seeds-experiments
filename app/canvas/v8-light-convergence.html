Core Direction
Direction
"Neighborhood coffee, not coffee brand." Position as the local roaster that ships — not a DTC brand pretending to be local. Every bag names the roaster, city, and the café that inspired the blend.
#core · locked
Reasons to Believe
Third-wave coffee already proved people will pay for provenance. This extends provenance to the roaster's story, not just the bean's origin.
#precedent
Subscription fatigue is real — but people don't cancel the subscriptions that feel personal. A named roaster creates that bond.
#retention
Logistics already support it — micro-roasters are overproducing. This gives them a distribution channel, not competition.
#supply
Risks
Quality inconsistency between roasters could undermine the brand. Need a curation bar, not just a marketplace.
#quality
If you scale too fast, "neighborhood" becomes performative. Growth rate needs to match the narrative.
#scale
Execution Plays
Launch in 3 cities only. One roaster per city. Name the subscription after the city, not the brand.
#launch
Each bag includes a postcard from the roaster — handwritten note about this particular batch. Analog in a digital channel.
#touchpoint
Instagram strategy: feature the roasters, not the product. The people ARE the brand. No lifestyle shots of mugs.
#content
Pricing: transparent breakdown on every bag. "Roaster gets X, shipping costs Y, we keep Z." Trust through math.
#pricing
⚡ What Would Break This?
⚡ Challenge
If Blue Bottle or Stumptown copies the model with better logistics, what's your moat? Distribution isn't defensible.
#competition
⚡ Challenge
"Neighborhood" implies small. What happens when you're in 50 cities? The metaphor breaks. Do you rebrand or cap growth?
