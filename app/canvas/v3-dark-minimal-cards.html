Sustainable fashion brand positioning
Fast fashion's real competitor isn't slow fashion — it's thrifting apps that make secondhand feel first-choice.
"Sustainability" is a dead word. People hear it and tune out. Find a new frame.
Repair cafes are booming in Berlin, Copenhagen, Tokyo. People want to maintain things, not just buy new ones. Durability as lifestyle.
Patagonia's "Don't Buy This Jacket" ad ran 13 years ago. What's the next evolution of that honesty?
The carbon footprint label is the new nutrition label. But nobody knows how to read it yet.
Rental fashion grew 10× in 3 years. Ownership is optional for under-30s.
Position as "clothes that get better." Not anti-new, not anti-fashion. Pro-aging. The opposite of planned obsolescence.
Brand voice: matter-of-fact, no guilt. Show the supply chain like a recipe, not a confession.
Every garment ships with a QR code linking to its production diary. Make the journey visible.
"Design for disassembly" — make every component separable and recyclable at end of life.
A "wear test" program — 100 people wear each piece for 6 months before it launches. Real data, not claims.
Partnership with local tailors in every city you sell in. Buy the garment, get lifetime alterations. The after-sale IS the product.
A "materials library" in-store. Customers touch, compare, learn. The shop is an education space.
What if you published the exact pollution cost of each garment and let customers offset it — or not?
Make returns painful on purpose. If you have to write why you're returning it, people think harder before buying.
Only release 4 pieces per year. Scarcity through restraint, not artificial drops. Anti-collection collection.
Never use the word "eco." It's greenwashing shorthand. Say exactly what you mean.
Don't position against fast fashion. Position for something. Negative framing attracts the wrong audience.
