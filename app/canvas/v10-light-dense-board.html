EV brand positioning vs Tesla
Tensions 8
Tesla owns "tech-forward EV." Competing on technology is a losing game. Find a different axis entirely.
Range anxiety is declining but charging UX is still terrible. The car is fine — the infrastructure is the blocker.
EVs are positioned as "responsible" purchases. Nobody gets excited about responsibility. Need an emotional hook.
The luxury EV segment is overcrowded — Lucid, Rivian, Mercedes EQ. Mid-market has more room.
Legacy automakers are catching up fast. The pure-EV advantage window is closing in 18 months.
Sustainability messaging is commodity. Every EV claims it. Zero differentiation potential.
Young buyers can't afford new EVs. The secondary market is where volume will come from.
EV design converged on the same aerodynamic blob. The cars are indistinguishable. Design is an opening.
Signals 7
BYD is winning on price in China and EU. The cost-value equation has shifted permanently.
Tesla brand sentiment is polarizing. A significant segment actively wants an alternative. Not anti-EV — anti-Tesla.
Rivian's adventure positioning proved EVs can own a lifestyle niche, not just a technology category.
Subscription models for car features are generating backlash. Ownership clarity is becoming a differentiator.
V2G (vehicle-to-grid) is approaching viability. The car as a home battery changes the value proposition entirely.
Volvo's safety legacy transferred cleanly to EV. Heritage brands have a narrative advantage over startups.
The "anti-Tesla" buyer exists and is growing: wants an EV, rejects the culture around Tesla.
Directions 6
"The anti-ego EV." Position against Tesla's personality cult. The car speaks for itself. No founder worship.
"Infrastructure-first." Don't sell a car — sell the charging network, the ownership ecosystem, the after-purchase experience.
"Design that doesn't apologize." Give it a visual identity so distinct people recognize it from a block away. Not another blob.
"Community-owned charging." Build the network with owners, not for them. Co-op model creates loyalty Tesla can't match.
Premium positioning with Scandinavian design language. (Too close to Polestar — not distinct enough.)
"Right-sized." Not the biggest battery, not the most range. The amount you actually need, priced accordingly.
Ideas 8
Test drive isn't at a dealership — it's a weekend rental through Turo partnership. Try it in your real life.
Transparent pricing page: exact cost of battery, motors, software. "Here's what you're paying for."
"Charge map" app that shows real availability, real speeds, real wait times. Solve the anxiety, not the range.
Trade-in program that guarantees battery value. Remove the depreciation fear that holds back EV adoption.
Launch with one model, one color, one config. Simplicity as a brand statement. The anti-configurator.
Partner with local mechanics for service. Not proprietary service centers. "Your mechanic can fix it."
Annual "owner summit" — a festival, not a shareholders meeting. Build the community in person.
Celebrity launch campaign. (Goes against anti-ego positioning.)
Modifiers 6
What if there was no app? Physical key, analog controls. Position against the over-digitized car.
What if you sold the battery separately? Car price drops 40%. Lease the battery. Totally new economics.
Constraint: no over-the-air feature unlocks. Everything the car can do, it does from day one. No paywalls.
What if you published a "things we got wrong" report annually? Radical honesty as brand ritual.
Never compare to Tesla in marketing. Comparisons elevate them. Be the thing people discover on their own.
Never use "sustainable" or "green." Show the numbers instead. Let the data do what adjectives can't.
